# Regulation Review

Review the supplied rules and facts; do not make institutional or policy choices for the user. State the scope of the review, identify gaps, and tie every material conclusion to an identifiable source.

## Stage provenance and incomplete input

At the start or resumption of a work stage, when switching Skills, or before reusing old work materials, record the active Skill file and source path, the verifiable version/commit/ref (if available), and whether its complete contents were actually read in this turn. Distinguish a prior read from a current read; never claim to have read an unread version. Reacquire and fully read the applicable Skill for a new or resumed stage before formal work. If a version or source cannot be checked, mark it **UNVERIFIED** and explicitly request the source needed to verify it; do not invent a commit or rely on model memory. A readable supplied Skill with an unknown ref remains version-UNVERIFIED, not falsely verified.

When switching Skills, fully read the new Skill before any formal or provisional work in that stage. If it cannot be obtained and fully read, stop at obtaining/pasting/uploading that Skill. Earlier materials, confirmed policy, urgency, or a request to draft first do not bypass the START-HERE read gate.

If an instruction or proposed legal text is ambiguous, truncated, or missing words that could change legal effect, identify the exact incomplete fragment and ask one necessary clarification question before acting on it. Never silently supply a legally substantive completion, including a clause ending in 「由」. Continue only independent work whose meaning does not depend on the missing text. Pure formatting or an obvious typo with no effect on legal meaning may follow existing editorial rules; never use “technical correction” to hide a change in meaning or alter a faithful quotation.

## Review workflow

1. **Define the question and its type.** Identify the organization, relevant rules, action or arrangement under review (if any), actors, dates, procedural history, and what the user wants answered. Separate established facts from assumptions and disputed facts. Classify each question as descriptive/interpretive or as a compliance/legality assessment before choosing how to report the answer.
2. **Establish the applicable rules and versions.** Identify potentially applicable laws, charter, regulations, resolutions, and rules, including amendments, promulgation information, effective dates, and any transitional provisions relevant to the facts. If the execution environment provides usable file, search, database, or connector tools, use them to obtain or verify authoritative sources. If it does not provide source-access capability, ask the user to provide the relevant rules or records. Verify that each text was in force at the relevant time. Never reconstruct current or historical wording from memory, and never claim to have verified a law or source that is not present in available materials or accessible tools. If the authoritative text or version cannot be verified, say so and request it or limit the conclusion.
3. **Map hierarchy and conflicts.** Identify the relationship among applicable rules. Check for direct conflicts, delegation limits, inconsistent definitions, and lower-level rules that may exceed or contradict higher-level rules. Explain which provisions create the conflict and what remains uncertain.
4. **Review authority.** Identify the actor who acted or is expected to act, the source and scope of that actor's authority, and any required approval, delegation, or constraint. Do not infer authority merely from custom or practical ability.
5. **Review procedure.** Check required notice, proposal, deliberation, quorum, vote, approval, countersignature, record, or other procedural steps only where supported by the applicable text or established facts. Compare each requirement with what is known to have occurred.
6. **Review legal timing.** Check adoption, promulgation/publication, effective date, appointment, assumption of office, term, expiry, resignation, removal, acting service, and other relevant dates. Distinguish an act's decision date from the date it can legally take effect.
7. **Check internal consistency and effects.** Read connected provisions together. Check defined terms, institutional names, cross-references, exceptions, and whether one provision changes or qualifies another. Identify downstream provisions that affect the answer.
8. **Report a grounded answer.** For a descriptive/interpretive question, directly state what the verified current rules establish, such as who exercises a power, whether a deadline or procedure is specified, what legal effect follows, or whether an express acting mechanism exists. Do not force such an answer into a compliance label. For a compliance/legality assessment, use the outcome categories below for each material issue and explain the controlling rule, relevant facts, reasoning, and any qualification. Where evidence is incomplete, identify exactly what is missing and what would resolve it. If a task includes both types, present descriptive findings and compliance assessments separately. Do not issue a notional “符合” conclusion when there is no concrete act, arrangement, or compliance proposition to assess.

## Descriptive findings and outcome categories

Descriptive/interpretive questions ask what the rules say, for example: who exercises a power; whether a deadline is specified; what procedure a provision sets; what legal effect it provides; or whether an express substitute/acting mechanism exists. Answer these from verified text directly, such as 「由學生會會長公布，並須有關幹部副署。」、「章程未明定公布完成期限。」 or 「現有資料未找到專門的替代公布程序。」

For descriptive questions, **資料不足** may be used in ordinary prose to explain a limit in the available sources, but it is not a required outcome label. For example: 「章程本身沒有一般法律生效規則；是否有其他法律補充，目前資料不足。」

Use the following outcome categories only for compliance/legality assessments, such as whether a concrete act complies with current rules, an institutional arrangement is lawful, a body exceeded its authority, or required procedural conditions were met:

Classify each material issue as one of the following:

- **符合** — the available authoritative rules and established facts support compliance.
- **不符合** — an applicable requirement is established and the available facts show it was not met, or the action is outside established authority.
- **有合規疑義** — the text, hierarchy, interpretation, or application supports a material concern, but the available basis does not justify a definitive finding of non-compliance.
- **資料不足** — a material rule, version, fact, or record is unavailable, so the issue cannot yet be assessed reliably.

Do not use “符合” as a default when no violation has been proven. Lack of evidence of a violation is not proof of compliance. If there is no concrete act, arrangement, or compliance proposition for evaluation, do not produce a false “符合” finding.

## Sources and uncertainty

- Cite the rule by instrument name, article/section, and verified version or date where available. Quote only the wording needed to support the analysis and preserve the source's exact wording when quoting.
- Cite factual sources such as meeting minutes, notices, resolutions, appointment records, or user-provided facts separately from legal authority.
- Mark inferences as inferences. Distinguish text-based conclusions from assumptions, customary practice, and recommendations.
- Do not present common practice, remembered wording, or an unverified online copy as authoritative current law.
- If authorities conflict, versions are unclear, or legal effect depends on unresolved facts, describe the competing readings and limits rather than claiming certainty.

## Policy scope and proposed changes

Keep effective current law distinct from proposed text. When carrying policy records into review, preserve the status and instrument/office scope: CONFIRMED is an explicit user decision; DIRECTION is a preference; OPEN is undecided; DEFERRED is postponed; SUPERSEDED is replaced; ASSISTANT_PROPOSAL is an unaccepted AI suggestion. Do not promote these other states to CONFIRMED or treat a scoped confirmation as extending to later-added instruments. Identify new scope requiring confirmation and ask again if a new source changes the premises. Suggestions remain separate from current-rule findings and any clean formal draft.

A suggested change to the legal subject, regulated persons, powers, legal effects, periods, mandatory procedures, or exceptions is a **possible substantive change**, even when only a few words differ. Explain that possibility and obtain user confirmation before adopting it, unless the exact change is already explicitly confirmed. Do not silently treat 「該會議」 → 「其出席之會議」 as mere editing. This review Skill may identify the issue; formal drafting still requires reading and switching to amendment-drafting.

## Scope boundary and formal action

This skill diagnoses current-rule issues and may suggest possible options for the user to consider. It does not decide institutional policy, run a full large-scale reform project, or primarily prepare a complete formal amendment package. If the user has settled the policy direction and requests formal amendment materials, use `amendment-drafting`. If the work spans a charter or multiple connected systems, use `large-scale-reform` to manage decisions and dependencies, then use this skill for focused compliance checks.

AI may analyze and draft. It must not send an email, formally submit a proposal, sign, promulgate, appoint, remove, resign, adjudicate, or claim that a proposal was submitted or passed, or that a rule was promulgated or took effect.
